# Produce Box Market Research — SEQ & Northern Rivers

**Scope:** Two parts. First, fruit-and-veg _box_ businesses operating across South East
Queensland and the Northern Rivers (NSW), benchmarked against Shift Green Fresh's own box
pricing — nine surveyed, pricing captured live from each business's own website in August 2026. Second, given deliveries are off the table except very close to the Varsity Lakes
warehouse, a closer look at the **physical shops a Varsity Lakes customer would actually
drive to instead** — Flannery's, Ambarella, and the Sunday organic farmers market, whose main
stall's shelf pricing this repo already has captured first-hand.

**A note on "doing well or not":** none of these businesses publish revenue or profit. What
follows is inferred from public signal only — years trading, review volume/score, still-live
vs. closed status, self-reported milestones. Treat it as directional, not audited.

---

## Headline numbers

- **7 of 9** surveyed businesses are delivery-first or delivery-only.
- **1 closure**: Scenic Rim Farm Box, Dec 2024, after $7.8M lifetime sales.
- **1 channel cut**: Farm Gate Express dropped home delivery entirely in 2024 to go
  pickup-only.
- **0 of 9** competitors publish a $/kg price — nobody in the set discloses total box weight.

## Two data points worth more than the rest

Most of the above is "still trading, seems fine" — a weak signal either way. These two are
the exceptions: one concrete failure, one concrete retreat, both in the same region as a
pickup-only box business.

### Scenic Rim Farm Box — closed, December 2024

Launched May 2020 as a COVID-stimulus initiative aggregating produce from 50+ Scenic Rim
farmers into one storefront and delivery run. By the time it closed in December 2024 it had
facilitated $7.8M in farmer-direct sales — not a small operation. The closure announcement
("an exciting and epic four years of operation") gives no financial or operational reason; it
reads as a deliberate wind-down rather than a collapse. No cause is confirmable from public
sources.

What _is_ confirmable: a multi-farm aggregator box model, in this exact region, did not carry
on past its pandemic-tailwind launch window despite genuine scale. Worth remembering against
the "source direct from many small farms" idea raised earlier — coordinating many small
growers is plausibly part of what got harder once the initial energy faded, not just demand.

### Farm Gate Express — cut home delivery, 2024

An established Hervey Bay operator with a physical shopfront, named supplier partnerships
(Hydroponic Harvest, Peterson Farms, Frog Hollow Mushrooms among others) and a multi-year
track record explicitly killed its home-delivery channel in 2024, stating plainly on their
site: _"NO HOME DELIVERY SERVICE IN 2024."_ They kept click-and-collect only.

That's a real business, with a shopfront to amortise costs against, concluding delivery
specifically didn't pencil — the same conclusion the transport modelling in the Business
Model tool keeps landing on for a solo operator with no shopfront at all. Pickup-only isn't
just a launch-stage compromise here; it's where a going concern in the same category retreated
to.

## The wider trade

Two macro figures worth holding against the box-level pricing above — neither is specific to
any business in the survey, both bear on whether this category has wind at its back or not.

- **−3.2%/yr** — IBISWorld's annualised revenue decline for Australian fruit & vegetable
  retailing over the five years to 2025–26 (to ~$4.1B industry-wide). Supermarket price
  competition and cost-of-living pressure are squeezing the category broadly, not just box
  subscriptions specifically.
- **5–10%** — Typical net margin food-subscription boxes land on generally, once a ~30% gross
  margin absorbs fulfilment, marketing and overhead. Thin enough that a churn spike or a
  fuel-price jump erases it — the reason logistics and labour get modelled this carefully in
  the Business Model tool rather than assumed away.

## What this means for the model

**Pickup-only has a real precedent, not just a launch excuse.** Farm Gate Express is an
established, shopfront-backed regional QLD operator that _gave up delivery_ in 2024. A newer,
shopfront-less operation choosing pickup-only from day one isn't cutting a corner — it's
starting where a comparable business ended up after testing the alternative.

**Current pricing sits at the cheap end of a legible field.** Where competitor pricing could
be captured at all, every tier of theirs sits at or above the equivalent Shift Green Fresh
box — Farm Gate Express's $42–$95 range is the closest, everyone else clusters $55–$136 for
boxes that mostly don't disclose weight. That's the organic-at-conventional-pricing strategy
already chosen, working as intended — and it's also the direct explanation for why the P&L
keeps coming out tight: the field is pricing itself for more margin, and this box mix is
deliberately not.

**Nobody publishes $/kg — that's an open differentiator.** None of the nine businesses states
total box weight, so shoppers in this category can't actually compare value across boxes
without doing their own guesswork. Being the one box in the region that says "$6.75/kg"
plainly is a small, cheap, and currently uncontested trust signal.

**Durability correlates with two things: years traded, and no lock-in.** The businesses with
the clearest long-run signal — FreshBox (14+ years, 3–5 year customer testimonials) and
Ripe n Raw (20+ years) — both lead with "pause, skip or cancel anytime." The multi-farm
aggregator that closed (Scenic Rim) and the one that cut delivery (Farm Gate Express) both
point the other way: coordination overhead and delivery logistics are where the model tends
to strain first, not customer demand.

## Local physical competition — the Varsity Lakes trade area

Pickup-only reframes the real competitive set. It isn't the nine delivery businesses above —
a Varsity Lakes customer isn't cross-shopping a Byron Bay delivery run. It's whatever they'd
otherwise drive to: Flannery's, Ambarella, and the Sunday organic market — whose main organic
stall, usefully, is a retailer this repo already has real photographed shelf pricing for,
rather than anything scraped.

### Who's actually shopping here

Varsity Lakes itself (postcode 4227) skews young family: median age 36, median weekly
household income $1,646 (2021 Census). The immediate cluster it sits in — Robina, Burleigh,
Merrimac, Mermaid Waters — reports $1,780/week median household income across 53,734 people;
Mermaid Beach–Broadbeach next door sits at $1,638/week with a median age of 40. Against the
2021 national median of roughly $1,746/week, this area is close to the middle overall, not
uniformly wealthy — the coastal pockets (Broadbeach, Mermaid Beach, Burleigh) pull the
cluster's average up, while Varsity Lakes proper is a comfortable, youngish family suburb
rather than a high-income enclave. "Good income, lots of young families" holds up; "boutique
prices without blinking" doesn't — this is a catchment that will notice a $10–15/kg organic
premium, not one that's immune to it.

### The two everyday walk-in shops

Neither is open only occasionally — both are a daily walk-in option, which is exactly what a
pickup-only box has to compete with on convenience, not just price.

### The Sunday certified-organic market — with real numbers attached

**Gold Coast Organic Farmers Market**, Merrimac State High School (Mermaid Waters), Sundays
6:00am–11:30am only. "Australia's first and only 100% Certified Organic farmers market" —
explicitly affordable-positioned, marketing itself against supermarket organic pricing rather
than as a boutique. Family-friendly, live music, community stalls. Of the three, this is the
closest positioning match to Shift Green Fresh — certified organic, affordable-not-boutique,
community-feeling — but it only runs 5.5 hours, once a week.

Its main organic stall is **Awesome Organics** — the board already photographed and ingested
in this repo (`data/retail-pricing/merrimac/`), matched line-by-line against the same product
catalog the box builder uses. One detail worth knowing about them specifically: the market's
own listing describes Awesome Organics as _"a reseller of fruits and vegetables — providing
produce that cannot be supplied by the local farmers"_ at the market, i.e. they're filling
gaps in what the certified-organic growers themselves bring, not necessarily growing
everything on their own board. That's a plausible explanation for a line like the carrot
outlier below — a resold item can carry a different price basis than a grower's own line.

Searching for Awesome Organics beyond that one Sunday stall turned up nothing — no separate
website, shopfront, or other market day found anywhere. That's not proof they don't trade
elsewhere, but it's consistent with what prompted this section: **the market is a fixed
6:00am–11:30am Sunday appointment, and the closest thing to a direct competitor at it doesn't
appear to be reachable any other way.** A pickup point open on more than one morning a week is
a real point of difference against that, not just a pricing one.

That still turns "the closest positioning match" from a qualitative read into an actual
item-by-item comparison against eco-farms wholesale cost:

_Median markup 1.47×, average 1.92× (the carrot line is a clear outlier pulling the average

The useful number here isn't any single row — it's the median. **1.47×** is what the region's
own certified-organic, affordably-positioned Sunday market — the single closest comparable
that exists — actually charges over eco-farms wholesale, once the carrot outlier is set
aside. Shift Green Fresh's boxes run at a 35% margin, a 1.54× markup over wholesale cost:
essentially the same multiple. The boxes aren't underpriced against the most direct local
comparison available; they're priced right at the middle of what it already charges, while
still needing to cover transport and packing labour a market stall doesn't itemise separately
(it's just folded into the same markup instead).

### What this means

**The two daily walk-in shops are documented as expensive, not just perceived that way.**
Flannery's and Ambarella both carry a public review record of "pricier than expected" — a box
priced near the Sunday market's own shelf-price median has real room to read as good value
against both, without needing to be the cheapest option in absolute terms.

**The Sunday market is the closest match in spirit — and now in price, not just positioning.**
Certified organic, affordably positioned, community-feeling, and (per Awesome Organics' own
board) pricing within a few cents of what a 35%-margin box already implies. What it doesn't
have is availability: 5.5 hours, one morning a week, and — as far as searching can tell —
Awesome Organics themselves aren't reachable any other way either. A pickup window offered on
more than one day, or outside a single 5.5-hour Sunday slot, isn't a minor convenience add-on
against this specific competitor — it's the one thing on offer here that they structurally
can't match.

**Nobody in this set is a "reserve online, collect a fixed box" business.** Flannery's and
Ambarella are daily browse-and-choose retail; the Sunday market is browse-and-choose too, just
once a week. That's a different shopping motion from all three, not just a different price
point — worth naming explicitly in any positioning, since it's a real gap in the immediate
cluster rather than a marketing claim.

**The carrot line is worth an in-person check.** A 5.1× markup on one item, sitting well
outside every other row, is more likely a board-reading or product-matching quirk than a true
market data point — before leaning on "we're 1.9× cheaper than the local organic market" as a
pitch, it's worth confirming that number in store rather than repeating it as-is.
